What the National Interest Waiver is

The EB-2 category covers members of the professions holding advanced degrees and individuals of exceptional ability. Ordinarily it requires a U.S. employer, a job offer and a certified labor certification. The National Interest Waiver asks USCIS to waive those requirements because doing so would be in the national interest, which turns the petition into a self-petition.

USCIS decides NIW petitions under the framework set out in Matter of Dhanasar, 26 I&N Dec. 884 (AAO 2016). The petition is won or lost on how precisely the proposed endeavor is defined and how directly the evidence supports each prong.

Who qualifies: the two-layer test

Layer 1 — the EB-2 baseline

Either an advanced degree (or a bachelor's degree plus five years of progressive post-baccalaureate experience), or exceptional ability in the sciences, arts or business shown through at least three of the regulatory criteria at 8 CFR 204.5(k)(3)(ii).

Layer 2 — the waiver

Meeting the degree requirement alone is not sufficient. The substance of the case is the endeavor and the evidence behind it.

The three Dhanasar prongs

1

Substantial merit and national importance

Two distinct tests in one prong. Merit is broad. National importance turns on the prospective impact of the specific endeavor — not the size of your employer or industry.

2

Well positioned to advance the endeavor

Education, skills, knowledge, record of success in related work, a model or plan, progress toward it, and the interest of others in your work.

3

On balance, waiving the job offer benefits the United States

Why it would be impractical to require a job offer and labor certification, or why the benefit of your contributions outweighs the labor-certification process.

Why the endeavor comes firstA vague endeavor cannot be assessed for national importance, and a vague endeavor is what an adjudicator sees when a petition simply describes a job title. We define it before anything else is drafted.

EB-2 NIW questions

Do I need a job offer or an employer to file an EB-2 NIW?
No. That is the defining feature of the National Interest Waiver. Because the job offer and labor certification requirements can be waived, an EB-2 NIW is a self-petition — you can file it yourself without an employer sponsoring you.
What is the difference between EB-1A and EB-2 NIW?
EB-1A requires sustained national or international acclaim. EB-2 NIW has a lower acclaim threshold but requires a nationally important endeavor and a reason to waive the job offer. They are different arguments, and some profiles support both.
Can you guarantee my petition will be approved?
No, and you should be cautious of anyone who does. USCIS adjudicates every petition on its own record, and no preparer controls that outcome.
